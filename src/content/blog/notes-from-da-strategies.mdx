I joined D+A Strategies as a quantitative trading trainee a few weeks ago. The association brings together students from Oxford, Cambridge, LSE, UCL, Imperial, Bocconi, and a long tail of other universities — focused on strategy development, macro research, and backtesting.

A few first impressions, with the caveat that I've only been here a short while.

## What I expected

I expected to mostly read papers and run notebooks. I expected the bar for "quant trading" inside a student association to be substantially lower than at a real prop firm.

## What's actually true

The bar is higher than I expected on the *engineering* side and roughly where I expected on the *finance theory* side.

People don't blink when you mention CVXPY, Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage, or walk-forward analysis. But the conversations about transaction-cost models, market microstructure, and execution algos are noticeably less developed than the conversations about factor research, macro indicators, and backtest methodology.

That makes sense — it's a research-and-strategy association, not a market-making one. But it shaped my mental model of where to invest my time.

## What I'm reading

Stuff I picked up from D+A peers in the first month:

1. **Lopez de Prado — *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*.** Required reading. The chapters on PSR, fractional differentiation, and meta-labeling all changed how I report results.
2. **Asness, Frazzini, Pedersen — *Quality Minus Junk* (2014).** A clean justification for the quality factor I was previously hand-waving.
3. **Almgren & Chriss — *Optimal Execution of Portfolio Transactions* (2000).** For when I get serious about transaction costs.

## What surprised me most

How willing senior members are to debate methodology. I posted a backtest claiming Sharpe 0.93 on the [factor portfolio](/projects/quant-factor-portfolio) and got pushed on:

- Sample size — am I statistically distinguishable from luck? (Now reporting PSR.)
- Survivorship bias — current S&P 500 ≠ historical S&P 500. (Now openly stated.)
- Sector exposure — was the alpha really cross-sectional, or was it a tech tilt? (Working on GICS neutralization.)

That feedback loop is worth more than the trading content itself.
